Gift Keeper Privacy

In plain words — no fine print tricks.

Last updated: November 9, 2026

What Gift Keeper keeps

Only what it needs to keep your gift list for you:

  • Your email address, so you can sign in. Your password is stored scrambled — nobody, not even Marybeth, can read it.
  • Your people: the names you add, and their birthdays and anniversaries if you add those.
  • Your gifts: what you bought for each person, who each gift is for, where you hid it, and how much you spent — only if you write those down. A photo of a gift, or of its shipping label, is kept right inside the list with that gift, if you add one.
  • Your list lives in the cloud: it is saved to your account (kept by Supabase, below), so it's there when you sign in on any phone, tablet, or computer — it is not only on one phone. This device also keeps a working copy so the app opens fast; where the two live is spelled out under "What stays on your device" below.
  • Your reminder settings: whether reminders are on, which dates they cover, and how many days ahead you picked. (One honest note: email reminders are still being finished — saving an email address in the reminder settings saves the address only, for now.)
  • A note you send through the "Send feedback" door in the app, and a photo too — but only if you choose to attach one (more on photos below).
  • If you use the screenshot reader (below): a fingerprint of the photo you asked it to read, and the words it read off it. The photo itself is not kept by the reader — the fingerprint and the words are kept so that reading the same photo again is free instead of costing another read.

Gift Keeper does not ask for your home address, and it never sees your bank or card numbers — payments are handled by Stripe (see Payments below).

Why it keeps it

So you always know what you bought, who it's for, and where you hid it — on every device you sign in with. That's the whole job. Your information is never sold, and it is never handed to advertisers. There are no ads and no trackers in Gift Keeper.

Who can see your list

Only you. Every account is walled off from every other account: when you sign in, you see your list and nobody else's. Marybeth cannot open your pages and read what's inside them.

Marybeth sees only what you send her:

  • A note you write through the "Send feedback" door.
  • A photo you choose to attach to that note, when a picture explains it better.

She can also see the email addresses on the approved list she manages — that's how the front door works — but never the inside of anybody's list.

Photos — only when you send one

There is exactly one way a photo reaches Marybeth: you pick it yourself on the Send feedback page and tap Send. A photo is never sent on its own, and a photo you send Marybeth is never read or scanned by anything — it goes to her eyes only.

  • Before it leaves your device, the photo is shrunk to a smaller copy.
  • It is kept in a private folder that only Marybeth can open, with the note you sent it with.
  • Marybeth gets an email with a link to it, and that link stops working after 7 days.

Reading a screenshot or label

The gift form has a "📸 Read a screenshot or label" button. That reading is separate from sending Marybeth a photo, and it only ever happens when you tap that button and pick a photo yourself:

  • The photo you pick is sent to Google's Gemini reader, which reads the words on it — the gift's name, the price, who it's for, the tracking number and carrier — and hands them back to the form.
  • The form shows you everything it filled in, and nothing is saved until you check it and tap Save gift yourself.
  • The reader never hands back home addresses — a shipping label's address block is left out on purpose. Names come back; street addresses never do.
  • The photo itself is not kept by the reader. Gift Keeper keeps a fingerprint of the photo and the words that were read, so reading the same photo again is free instead of costing another read.
  • There is a limit of 60 new photo reads a day per account, to keep the reading costs sane.
  • Reading is always your choice. The form works exactly the same filled in by hand, and a photo that won't read never stops you saving the gift.

Payments

Gift Keeper costs $15.99 a year. It is a subscription: it renews once a year until you cancel, and there are never any ads. Checkout is handled by Stripe, a large payments company. Your card number goes to Stripe, not to Gift Keeper — Gift Keeper never sees it or stores it. Stripe tells Gift Keeper that you paid, and that is what opens your account.

Refunds: if Gift Keeper doesn't work for you, ask within 14 days of a payment and you'll get that payment back — that's the promise in the Terms. Ask through Send feedback in the app, or by email.

Cancelling is always your call: ask the same way — Send feedback in the app, or email — and Marybeth will stop it for you, or use the subscription link in the receipt emails Stripe sends you. Cancelling stops the next yearly charge; Gift Keeper stays open for you until the year you paid for runs out.

The helpers Gift Keeper runs on

Gift Keeper is a small, hand-run place, and it stands on a few bigger services. Each one only gets what it needs to do its one job:

  • Supabase — keeps your account and your gift list in its database, behind your sign-in.
  • Stripe — runs the $15.99-a-year subscription, so card numbers never touch Gift Keeper.
  • Resend — sends the emails, like your welcome note and the photo links Marybeth gets when you send her a photo.
  • Google Gemini — reads the words off the screenshots and label photos you ask it to read on the gift form. It gets only the photo you picked for that read, and it never hands back home addresses.
  • GitHub Pages — serves the Gift Keeper pages themselves.

What stays on your device

Gift Keeper keeps a few things in your own browser, on your own device:

  • Your gift list itself is kept on the device too, as a working copy, so the app opens fast. The real home of your list is your account in the cloud — that's why your list looks the same on your phone, tablet, and computer, and it's why your list is still there if you sign in on a new phone. If the two ever disagree, the account copy wins.
  • Your sign-in, so you don't have to type your password every time you open it.
  • Your screen-lock PIN, if you turn the optional screen lock on. The PIN never leaves the device, and it is never sent to your account. The lock is off unless you turn it on, and it only locks that one device.

Because a working copy lives on the device, signing out does not wipe the device copy — anyone holding your unlocked phone could still open the browser and see it. If that worries you, the backup button in Settings lets you save a copy as a file first, and the delete door (next sections) wipes the device copy along with the account.

How long things are kept

Your list is kept for as long as your account is open — that's the point of the place. If you delete your account (next section), your gift list, your saved photo reads (the fingerprints and read words, which are tied to your sign-in and are removed with it), your photo notes and the photos themselves, your feedback notes, and your sign-in are deleted with it. One honest exception: emails that were already sent — like a photo you sent Marybeth — can still sit in an inbox afterwards, the same way any email you send stays in the other person's mailbox.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account yourself, any time, right inside Gift Keeper:

  • Open Settings and look for Delete my account, under your sign-out button.
  • It asks you twice, on purpose, and you type your email to be sure.
  • Deleting removes your gift list, your saved photo reads, your sent photo notes and photos, your feedback notes, and your sign-in, for good — including the copy on the device you're using. It can't be undone.

Two things deleting does not do: it does not by itself refund a payment, and it does not stop your $15.99-a-year subscription — the yearly charge keeps renewing until the subscription is cancelled in Stripe. To cancel, tap 💬 Send feedback in the app (or email Marybeth) and she'll stop it for you, or use the subscription link in the receipt emails Stripe sends you. Refunds are Marybeth's call the same way: the Terms promise still stands — ask within 14 days of a payment and you'll get that payment back.

If you'd rather not use the button, tap 💬 Send feedback and ask — Marybeth will delete your account for you.

If this page changes

If the way Gift Keeper handles your information ever changes, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change with it. Big changes will be told plainly, in the app or by email — not slipped in quietly.
